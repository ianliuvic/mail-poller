# Fabric Guide

Most swimwear is made from one of two fabrics — nylon/spandex or polyester/spandex. Here is what they are, how they differ, and how to specify fabric for your order.

## Key fabric attributes

These are the attributes we check first when confirming a fabric — and the details to send us if you are specifying one.

- CompositionNylon or polyester blended with spandex (elastane). Spandex provides stretch and shape recovery — around 15–18% is typical for swimwear.

- WeightStandard swimwear fabric is about 200 GSM. Most styles work at 200 GSM; lighter or heavier options (roughly 180–230 GSM) are possible depending on the design.

- Knit structureWe mainly use warp-knit swimwear fabric — it stretches evenly and keeps its shape through wear and washing.

- StretchSwimwear should have four-way stretch (length and width) so it moves with the body and recovers its shape.

- SurfaceOur standard finish is matte. Other surfaces — slight shine, peach-skin or textured — are available on request.

- PerformanceColor fastness to chlorine, sunlight and sweat, plus shrinkage and pilling, can be confirmed to standard test methods when your market requires it. Request a test report

## Common swimwear fabrics

Two fabrics cover most swimwear programs — around 80–90% of what we produce. Both are matte, around 200 GSM and available in a wide range of solid colors.

Fabric | Composition | Best for | Notes
Nylon / spandex | 82% nylon, 18% spandex | Solid colors | Soft hand feel and good recovery, with hundreds of in-stock colors. Not used for sublimation prints.
Polyester / spandex | 85% polyester, 15% spandex | Prints and solid colors | Better chlorine and sunlight resistance, holds prints well, and is usually a little cheaper than nylon.

**How to choose:** pick nylon/spandex for solid colors and a softer, premium hand feel. Pick polyester/spandex for prints, or when durability and value matter most — see the color & print guide for how each fabric prints.

## Special fabrics

Eco-friendly fabrics, spandex-free options, premium imported fabrics and jacquard or textured fabrics are usually made to order rather than held in stock.

- **Eco-friendly and spandex-free fabrics** — generally not stocked. Suppliers may only carry greige goods (undyed fabric), and custom dyeing has a high minimum — see the color guide.

- **Premium imported fabrics** (e.g. Italian mills such as Carvico, Eurojersey Sensitive®, Iluna and Penn Italia) — sourced from abroad, so the cost is significantly higher. Domestic near-matches exist but are also made to order.

- **Jacquard and textured fabrics** — not stocked, but we keep color cards and small samples of common styles; available colors are not always fixed.

Need a specific fabric? We can source it for you — see our fabrics & trims sourcing page.

## How to specify

Tell us the composition, weight (GSM), knit structure, stretch and surface you need, and send a color reference. If you already have a physical reference, sending us a **swatch or sample swimsuit** is the fastest and most accurate way to match it — images help, but they cannot replace a physical sample.

Stock fabrics can be confirmed quickly. Special fabrics usually need to be ordered, which affects minimums and lead time — we will confirm what is possible before you commit.

## Common questions

 Why don't you use cotton for swimwear?
Cotton absorbs water, holds it against the skin and loses its shape when wet. Swimwear fabrics are quick-drying and stretchy with good recovery — that is why nylon and polyester blends with spandex are the standard.
  Nylon or polyester — which should I choose?
For solid colors and a softer hand feel, choose nylon/spandex. For prints, or when chlorine/sunlight resistance and value matter most, choose polyester/spandex.
  Do you keep fabric in stock?
Our standard fabrics are stocked in a wide range of solid colors. Special fabrics — eco, spandex-free, imported or textured — are usually made to order.
  Can you print on nylon?
Sublimation printing works best on polyester, so nylon is mostly used for solid colors — see the color & print guide for details.
  Are eco-friendly fabrics certified?
When your market requires certification, we can confirm what is available for the fabric you choose — ask us before production.
